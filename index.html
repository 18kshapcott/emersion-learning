What makes EMeRsion different?

  • 1 Learners actively request information instead of receiving the entire patient case upfront.
  • 2 Cases integrate medications, laboratory values, patient interviews, physical findings, functional status, and other clinically relevant information.
  • 3 Learners identify clinical problems and develop individualized treatment and monitoring plans.
  • 4 Formative feedback highlights collected information, missed clinical elements, and recommended care plans.

About the Platform

A learner-driven approach to virtual patient care

Traditional patient cases often provide learners with all available information at once. EMeRsion instead requires learners to decide what information is necessary, request that information, interpret the findings, and use the collected data to develop a care plan.

The platform is designed to simulate the information-gathering and decision-making processes used by pharmacists during patient care. Cases may include outpatient, community, and inpatient clinical settings.

Preceptor Pilot

What you will be asked to do

Thank you for participating in the EMeRsion Learning preceptor pilot. Your experience and feedback will help refine the platform and evaluate its potential use in pharmacy and experiential education.

Step 1

Launch EMeRsion

Open the case platform and review the available virtual patient cases.

Step 2

Complete at least one patient case

Select a case and request the clinical information you would need to evaluate the patient.

Step 3

Submit a care plan

Identify the patient's primary and secondary problems, then provide treatment and monitoring recommendations.

Step 4

Review the generated feedback

Review the problem breakdown, critical elements, teaching points, and recommended care plan.

Step 5

Complete the pilot survey

After exploring the platform, provide feedback about its usability, educational value, realism, and potential use in experiential education.

Information to explore

During the case, try requesting multiple types of patient information. The system is designed to respond to common clinical questions and abbreviations.

  • Past medical history
  • Home medications
  • Laboratory values
  • Vital signs
  • Physical examination
  • Patient interview factors
  • Functional status
  • Intake and output
  • Family and social history
  • Diagnostic information

Tips for exploring EMeRsion

Ask specific questions

Instead of requesting all laboratory results, ask for a specific value such as serum creatinine, potassium, A1c, LDL cholesterol, or ejection fraction.

Interview the patient

Ask about medication adherence, cost, treatment preferences, adverse effects, home monitoring, and barriers to care.

Think before submitting

Prioritize the patient's problems and include specific, actionable treatment and monitoring recommendations.
